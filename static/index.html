Pay by tag

Archived offers are excluded, like every other chart on this page -- this tracks the active pipeline, not a retrospective pay archive (the "📊 Pay stats" popup in the Jobs tab is the one place that still includes archived offers, by design). "Untagged" and the 5 tags hidden by default in the Jobs tab (Fellowship, Internship, Physicist, Post-Doc, Professorship) are excluded here too. Best-effort: remuneration is either scraped (title/excerpt only, so most listings won't have one) or entered by hand via a job card's "+ Pay" / "✎" button. Figures are always normalized to an annual amount first -- a per-month entry is multiplied by 12 before averaging. Each histogram bins that tag's own offers into fixed 500-wide pay ranges (its own min–max, not a shared scale across tags), with a normal (Gaussian) curve fit to the same mean/std dev overlaid -- how closely the bars hug the curve is itself informative (a tight fit suggests pay here genuinely clusters around one typical figure; a poor fit suggests several distinct pay tiers lumped under one tag). Variance/std dev need at least 2 values to mean anything and show "—" otherwise.

Pay by city

Mean pay per city (only cities with at least one parseable pay value are shown). No curve here -- city is a category with no natural order, same reasoning as Offers by tag/Volume by source below.

Offers by tag

Same tag exclusions as Pay by tag above ("Untagged" plus the 5 tags hidden by default in Jobs). How many job/thesis offers fall under each tag in total, archived included. No curve fit here -- tags are categories with no natural order, so there's no continuous axis for a curve to follow the way there is for a histogram or a time series.

Volume by source

How many offers each job source has contributed in total. Same "no curve, categories aren't ordered" reasoning as Offers by tag.

Offers over time

Monthly count of offers *dated* that month (each offer's own pub_date -- its listed posting date, or "now" as a fallback for a source with no real date). A quadratic trend curve (least-squares fit, not a moving average) is overlaid to show the broad direction independent of month-to-month noise.

New jobs over time

Monthly count of offers this app *first scraped* that month (first_seen_at -- never defaulted or backfilled, always the real discovery moment) -- a different question from "Offers over time" above: this tracks the scraper's own discovery activity, not when postings themselves claim to be dated. The two can disagree a lot for a source with real (possibly old) listing dates.
